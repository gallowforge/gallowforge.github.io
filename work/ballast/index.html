Ballast

Personal finance that turns savings into a salary that lasts.

Why it exists

Living on savings is a guessing game. A banking app shows a balance and a budgeting app assumes a paycheck. Neither says how much is safe to spend today, or whether the money lasts until the date you need.

What it does

Ballast pays you a fixed salary from your savings, keeps aside the bills still to come, and gives one number: what is safe to spend today. It draws how long the savings last as a road ending on a date, and finds the largest salary that still reaches a date you set.

Bank statements are read in the browser. There is no account or server, and it works offline.

How it is built

  • Money as whole numbers

    Every amount is an integer of the currency's smallest unit. Exchange rates are decimal strings applied with big-integer arithmetic and rounded once, so converting between currencies uses no floating point.

  • Every row against the balance

    Each statement row's balance must equal the previous balance plus its amount. Rows that do not follow are listed, usually because a column was matched wrongly.

  • The browser enforces the privacy

    Every page carries a Content Security Policy, a browser-enforced rule, that allows connections only to the site itself.

  • Calculations apart from screens

    Safe to spend, the month between paydays, budgets and the runway are pure functions with tests beside them, checked without a browser.

What was hard

A comma with two meanings

Amounts are whole numbers once stored, so the risk sits where typed text becomes a number. My first parser removed every comma as a thousands mark. Someone who writes decimals with a comma typed 1,5 and got 15. An independent review found it.

The fix treats a comma as a thousands mark only when it groups three digits. A second review found what I had left: with three decimal places, 0,500 still read as five hundred, a thousand times too much. A thousands group cannot start with zero, so that comma is now a decimal point. Both cases now have tests.

Limits

  • It cannot read scanned statements, which are pictures with no text, and says so.
  • Nothing syncs: another device means exporting a backup and restoring it.
  • A typed 1,500 still means fifteen hundred, so a three-place comma decimal needs a point instead.
  • The runway is arithmetic on the figures entered, and it looks ten years ahead.
